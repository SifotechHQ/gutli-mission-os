// The Story

Who is
Gutli Man?

Jasmeet Singh Arora, 53, from Kolkata. Not a farmer. Not a botanist. Just a man who couldn't sleep after watching Bengal's ancient mango orchards disappear — and the farmers who depended on them earn ₹2,000 for an entire season's work.

In 2017, he had one idea: plant mango trees using discarded seeds. Mango trees don't just give fruit — they store one ton of CO₂ in a lifetime, shelter birds, restore ecosystems, and live for 100 years. One grafted tree earns a farmer ₹300–400 every year. That's 15x more than paddy. For 40 years.

The breakthrough was grafting — taking a scion branch from a proven fruiting tree and joining it to a new seedling. What normally takes 15 years to fruit now takes 3. Of every 1,000 seeds collected, Jasmeet's lab nurtures 100 survivors, grafts them over 6 months, and hands each sapling to a farmer who owns it forever.

In May 2023, he posted one video asking for one seed. It went viral. Since then: 2.1 million seeds collected. 800,000 trees distributed. 5,000+ families in Sundarbans, Bankura, Purulia and Jharkhand with a new income stream — at zero government cost.

"If every Indian gives just one mango pit, we can plant 200 million trees in a single season — more than the entire population of Australia."
— Jasmeet Singh Arora · Gutliman of India
